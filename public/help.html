Getting started

How do I sign in?

Go to glowtape.net, enter your name, email, and your code, and tap Email me a sign-in code. A 6-digit code arrives by email — type it in and you're in. There is no password to remember, ever. Verified your cell number in the app? Tap Text me a code to get your sign-in code by text instead.

Did your show already put your cell number on your role? Then you can skip the code hunt: tap Text me a code, type the code, then confirm your name, email and age range. That makes your account and puts you in your role (or, for a parent, connects you to your child) in one go.

First time? Creating an account needs a code: the join code from your production, a role code from your director, or a community code from the organizer. If you used a production or role code, you land straight in your show. Signing back in later needs no code.

Can I sign in with my fingerprint or face?

Yes, once you've set it up on that phone or computer. Sign in with a code first, then in ⚙ Settings (top of your Home page) find Fingerprint sign-in and tap Set up fingerprint sign-in on this device. Your phone asks for your fingerprint, face, or screen-unlock PIN — that's it. Next time, tap Sign in with fingerprint or face on the sign-in page. Works with iPhone (Face ID / Touch ID), Android fingerprint, Mac Touch ID, and Windows Hello.

Your fingerprint never leaves your device; Glow Tape never sees it. (This uses "passkeys", the standard Apple, Google and Microsoft built for this.) Set it up on each device you use. Lost a phone? Remove it from the same list with ✕. The emailed code always still works.

Can I change my email address?

Yes — in ⚙ Settings (top of your Home page), find My email and tap Change email. A confirmation code goes to the new address; type it in and your account switches to it. Sign in with the new address from then on.

The code email didn't come.

Check your spam or junk folder — the sender is callboard@glowtape.net. Adding that address to your contacts fixes it permanently. Emailed codes work for 2 hours, so a slow inbox is fine; after that (or 5 wrong tries), just request a fresh one.

How do I join my show?

Your director or stage manager will give you a 6-letter join code (at the read-through, or by text/email). On your Glow Tape home screen, type it under Join a production and tap Join. That's it — you'll see the show's schedule immediately.

Can I put Glow Tape on my home screen like an app?

Yes — and you can do it before you sign in: the sign-in page works fine (it also has these steps under 📲 Put Glow Tape on your home screen).

iPhone or iPad:

  1. Open glowtape.net in Safari. (If you tapped a link inside the Google, Gmail or Facebook app, you're in that app's browser — open Safari and type glowtape.net instead.)
  2. Tap the Share button (the square with an arrow pointing up). On newer iPhones it's tucked behind the ••• button at the bottom right — tap that, then Share.
  3. Scroll down the list that pops up and tap Add to Home Screen. Don't see it? At the very bottom tap View More or Edit Actions… — it's in there.
  4. Tap Add, then open Glow Tape from its new icon.

Android: open glowtape.net in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu, tap Add to Home screen (or Install app).

It works fine in a regular browser too — this is optional.

Some boxes look like they're already filled in?

Gray slanted writing inside a box is just an example of what you might write — usually it starts with "Example:". The box is still empty; the gray text disappears the moment you start typing. Anything you've actually entered shows in straight, dark text.

The Dashboard (your landing page)

Opening a show lands you on the Dashboard — greeted with Tonight! Tonight! — what's in front of you right this moment. A countdown to opening night with a strip of glow tape being laid down toward it (it doesn't start at zero — by the time you're reading this you've already auditioned, been cast, and signed in), the next event you're called to with its place and timeline and the Got-it button right there, a short "needs you" list (line notes, to-dos, sign-up sheets you haven't booked), any open poll a manager pinned (you can vote right there), the pinned announcement, and a quote of the day — the whole cast sees the same one, and directors can add their own favorites from the show in Manage → Dashboard quotes. New to the show? A little "getting settled" checklist walks you through notifications, conflicts, the schedule, and your bio, then gets out of the way.

How do I get around?

Tap ☰ at the top left of any page. The menu has a heading for each of your shows, one for Around Grays Harbor (the community board, calendar, and open auditions), and one for your own settings and profile — so you can jump from anywhere to anywhere. Tap a heading to open or close it; the little ▸ next to Schedule or Manage opens a list of that page's sections. The button shows which part of the show you're on.

Long pages fold, too: sections start closed, so a page reads like a table of contents — tap a heading to open or close it. Your phone or computer remembers which ones you keep open. Long lists fold into groups that start closed — the schedule list by week, To-Do by department, Notes by month, the show breakdown by act — and each group's heading tells you what's inside (how many events, which days, how many still need your "Got it") so you only open the one you need.

What are all the parts of a show?

Inside a production: Schedule — every rehearsal and performance, your conflicts, calendar links. Messages — announcements and group channels. To-Do — task lists by department, plus your program-bio box when one's been requested. Sheets — the show breakdown (your songs/scenes) plus props, costumes, set, and cue trackers. Docs — scripts, scores, tracks, and links; PDFs open with notes and a highlighter. Notes — rehearsal notes anyone can read. People — who's who in the show (contact details are for managers only). Managers also see Casting and Manage.

For cast & crew

What does "Got it 👍" do?

It tells your stage manager you've seen that rehearsal or change. Tap it on every event you're called to — it saves them from chasing people down. If an event's time or place changes, you'll get an email and your "Got it" resets so you can confirm you saw the new plan.

What are conflicts?

Days you're not available — work trips, weddings, anything. Enter them under My conflicts on the Schedule tab as early as possible, so the schedule gets built around them instead of colliding with them. Standing commitments are one entry: set Repeats to every week, every other week, or monthly by day of the week — "city council, 2nd and 4th Mondays" is exactly what that last one is for. Pick which weeks, set Repeat until (usually closing night), and every date is added in one go; they show as one 🔁 line you can remove all at once if the commitment ends. Work or class hours are different — you're busy part of the day but free after: use ⏰ My weekly busy hours instead. Tap the days, enter the hours (say, Mon–Fri 9:00am–5:00pm for work), and the managers are warned only when an event actually overlaps those hours — an evening rehearsal on a workday raises no flag at all. Your conflicts — and the reasons you type in — are visible only to you and the managers, not the rest of the cast.

My kid has soccer on Tuesdays — how do I enter that?

If you've claimed a child's role, the conflict form on the Schedule tab grows a "Who is this for?" picker — choose the child instead of Me, then enter dates, repeating commitments, or weekly busy hours exactly as you would for yourself. Their conflicts count in the managers' scheduling tools just like everyone else's, and either parent can see, add, or remove them.

How do I get notifications on my phone?

In ⚙ Settings (top of your Home page), find Notifications and tap 🔔 Turn on notifications — messages, schedule changes, and task pings then arrive like any other app's notifications. It's per-device, so turn it on on both your phone and computer if you like. iPhone: add Glow Tape to your Home Screen first (Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen — step-by-step) and open it from there — Apple only allows notifications for installed apps. Muted channels never ping you.

How do I get text reminders?

In ⚙ Settings (top of your Home page), find Text reminders, add your cell number, and type in the 6-digit code that arrives by text. From then on you'll get a text before each call — about 10 hours ahead and again about 2 hours out, unless you pick different times (see below) — never between 9pm and 7am (an early-morning call is announced the evening before instead). Reply STOP anytime to turn them off, or un-check the box in the app.

Can the managers text me?

Yes, if you've turned on text reminders. The same yes covers urgent messages your show's managers send to the whole cast, like a room change or a cancellation: no more than 3 a day, never between 9pm and 7am. If you don't get texts from Glow Tape, the same message reaches you as a private Glow Tape message (in your 🔒 channel with the managers, which your parent or guardian can see too) and by email. Reply STOP anytime.

Can I choose when I'm reminded?

Yes. In ⚙ Settings (top of your Home page), under When to remind me, tap up to three: the night before (7pm), the morning of (8am), or 10 hours, 4 hours, 2 hours, 1 hour, or 30 minutes before. The same times are used for your phone notifications and your texts (if you pick three, the earliest arrives as a notification only — texts stay at two per call). Quiet hours still win: nothing arrives between 9pm and 7am, so a reminder that would land then comes the evening before (or just before 9pm) instead. Tap them all off if you don't want reminders — you'll still get the schedule emails. Guardians are reminded at their own chosen times for their children's calls.

The schedule is a lot to scroll. Can I see just this week or this month?

Yes — cast open the Schedule tab on the Month calendar, and you can switch with the List / Week / Month buttons at the top (List is the long scrolling view, and where directors and stage managers start). Each event shows just its name, type, time and your Got it button — tap the event's name (or ▸ More) for the place, who's called, notes and calendar links. When the whole cast is called it simply says Full cast. Week shows seven days at a time; use ◂ and ▸ to move, and This week jumps back. Month is a calendar: tap any day that has something on it and that day's details open underneath. Tap 🙋 Just my calls to hide rehearsals you're not called to (guardians see their children's calls too). Glow Tape remembers your choices on that phone or computer.

How do I join a rehearsal or meeting on Zoom?

Events that meet online carry a 💻 Online tag (or 💻 + in person when some people are in the room). Tap 💻 Join the video call — it's on the Dashboard's Next up card, on the event in the Schedule tab (right on the folded card from half an hour before), in the schedule email, in your text and app reminders, and in the calendar entry if you added it to your own calendar. Any video service works: Zoom, Google Meet, Teams…

Can I print my schedule?

Yes — on the Schedule tab, tap 🖨 Print my schedule for a paper-friendly page with a chronological list and monthly calendar grids of just your calls. Behind on "Got it"s? 👍 Got them all acknowledges every upcoming event at once (it asks first — only use it if you've really read them).

What's the community calendar?

Home → Around Grays Harbor → Community board & calendar: every audition and performance across all Grays Harbor productions on one calendar, plus the community message board. It opens showing this month and next; All dates shows everything scheduled, and Hide tucks the calendar away. Rehearsals and everything else stay private to each show.

Can I see rehearsals on my own calendar?

Yes, two ways. On any event, tap + Google Calendar or + Apple / other calendar to add that one. Or, at the bottom of the Schedule tab, tap Get my calendar link and subscribe once — every event you're called to appears in your calendar and stays up to date. (Google can take a few hours to refresh subscribed calendars; the app and texts are always current.)

What's my profile for?

Your community profile (Home → My profile) is your acting résumé: a stage-history table (year, company, show, role), skills, an optional headshot. The Company column is a dropdown of local companies; pick ✏️ Somewhere else… to type any other name. It travels with you from show to show, anyone signed in can see it, and directors see it next to your audition signups. It's also right inside every audition form — edit it there and signing up saves your changes too. All of it is optional. (Members under 18 don't upload headshots — directors take photos at auditions if needed.)

How do auditions work?

When a director opens auditions, the show appears under Auditions on everyone's home screen and on the community calendar (with a signup link). The form shows what the show is about, who's running it (director, stage manager…), the audition dates and places, the roles on offer as checkboxes — each with the director's casting notes on what the part calls for (check everything that appeals — it's interest, not a commitment) — every performance date (plus strike, if scheduled), your conflicts (dates plus a word on why, just like the Schedule tab), and the director's questions. Signing up asks you to confirm you're available for all performances and for strike — that part is a commitment, and directors cast on it. You can come back and update your answers any time until auditions close — and one tap puts every audition time on your phone's calendar.

Where are the rehearsal tracks / scripts / forms?

On your show's Docs tab — the managers post documents and links there (scripts, rehearsal tracks, character breakdowns, forms). Audition materials appear right on the audition signup form.

Reading the script with notes (the script room)

When a manager posts a PDF — script, libretto, score — it appears on the Docs tab with a 📖 Read with notes link. Pick a tool and use the page: 📝 Note pins a note to the exact spot you tap, ✏️ Draw is a red pencil, 🖍 Highlight is a highlighter (pick your shade — yellow, cyan, lime green, pink — and your style: ✍ freehand, 📏 straight lines that level themselves, or ▭ a box that washes a whole block at once), and 🧽 Erase removes your own marks — tap the ones you want gone (a dashed outline marks them), then erase them all at once. ↩ Undo takes back your last note or mark, no questions asked. Everything you make is private to you — blocking, breath marks, underlines — unless it's from a manager, whose 📌 notes and marks everyone sees ("pencil in the cut at the top of this page"). Notes can be checked off when taken care of, and the list under the page jumps you straight to any note. Know the tools already? ⛶ Compact view shrinks everything to icons so the page gets the whole screen (it remembers your choice). Your personal notes and marks are never visible to anyone else — not even the director.

Highlight my lines / going off book

In the script room, tap ✨ My lines and every one of your character's speeches lights up on the page — the app reads the script's own text and follows the speaker names (it's filled in from your cast assignment; edit it if your script spells it differently, or list several names separated by commas). When it's time to memorize, tap 🙈 Off book instead: your lines get covered with gray bars while everyone else's stay readable. Read your cue, say your line out loud, then tap the bar to peek and check yourself — tap again to cover it back up, and turning the page covers everything again. It's honest flashcards, built into the script. Works on scripts with standard NAME-then-dialogue formatting (a scanned photocopy has no text to read, so there it stays quiet); nothing about it is saved or visible to anyone else.

What's a line note?

The stage manager's classic paperwork, without the paper. During a run-through, someone who manages the show follows along in the script room with the 🎯 Line note tool: tap the line, pick the actor and what happened — Dropped line, Paraphrased, Skipped ahead, Jumped a cue, or Called for line — and save it. Nobody's phone buzzes during the run: notes collect quietly, and when the team is done for the night, one tap on 📣 Send tonight's line notes sends each actor a single notification covering all of theirs. Every note waits at the top of their To-do tab, quoting the printed lines around the spot (when the script is a real digital PDF rather than a scan), with a Show me in the script link that opens the script right to the 🎯 pin marking the exact line. From there, ◂ Previous and Next ▸ step through every open note in page order, and fixing one flows straight to the next — no bouncing back and forth. Once they've got the line down, they check it off — ✓ Got it — it's fixed — and the managers see it's handled. Line notes are coaching, not a scoreboard: each actor sees only their own (parents see their child's), and only the managers see the whole list.

How do video check-ins work?

Sometimes the managers want to hear or see something before the next rehearsal, like your dialect lines, a song cut, or a monologue. They send a video request, you get an email and a notification, and the request waits in your To-do tab under 🎥 Video check-ins. Tap 🎥 Record: Glow Tape opens your phone's front camera (your browser asks to use the camera and microphone the first time), you tap ● Start recording, and it stops by itself at 3 minutes. Watch it back, then ✓ Send this one or ↺ Record again. You can also upload a video you already have, as long as it's under 3 minutes. Sending a new one later replaces the old one.

Who sees it: only you (and your parents, for a young performer), the managers, and the reviewers the managers picked (for example a dialect coach in the show's Production team group). Castmates never see each other's videos. When a reviewer sends notes, you get an email and they show up right under your video.

For managers: ask from To-do → 🎥 Video check-ins → Ask for a video, choose every performer or pick people, and choose who else can review. Reviewers don't need Manage access, just a spot in the group you pick. Videos are big, so they are not included in Glow Tape's backups. Delete them once you've used them (🗑 Delete all videos), and archiving the show deletes them automatically.

Can I message the managers privately?

Yes. In your show's Messages tab, tap 🔒 Message the managers privately. That opens a channel between you and all the show's managers — director, stage manager, and the rest — for anything you'd rather not post to the full cast (a costume worry, a scheduling crunch, a question). It's semi-private on purpose: there are never one-on-one messages on Glow Tape, and parents of young members see their child's team channel too.

Checking in at rehearsal (the door QR)

If your stage manager has a check-in QR by the door (or up on their phone), scan it with your phone's camera and tap the big ✋ I'm here button — that's your whole check-in. Parents get a button for each kid they manage. It marks you present with your arrival time so the stage manager doesn't have to hunt you down backstage. It only works around the event's actual time, so yesterday's poster can't check you in today.

Booking a fitting (sign-up slots)

When the costumer (or any manager) needs people one at a time, a sign-up sheet appears under 📐 Sign-up slots on the Schedule tab: costume fittings, headshots, whatever. Tap an open time to book it — tap a different time on the same sheet to move yourself, or your own time to cancel (an ↩ Put it back undo appears in case that was a slip). Parents pick which kid they're booking for first. Everyone can see who has which time, so trading is easy. Managers: post a sheet with + New sign-up sheet — name it, pick the day, the hours, and how many minutes each person gets, and the slots generate themselves (post a second day under the same name and they merge into one sheet).

The cast-party sign-up (who's bringing what)

When an event has a 🍪 bring-something list (a manager turns it on when setting up a cast party or potluck), the event shows who's bringing what, grouped by category — Mains, Sides, Desserts, Drinks, or whatever the managers chose. Type what you'll bring, pick the category, tap I'll bring it, and your cookies are on the record under Desserts. Everyone sees the list, which is the point: no five bags of chips and zero desserts. Remove your own item any time if plans change.

RSVP for the cast party

Cast parties and potlucks ask 🎉 Are you coming? right on the event card (and on your Dashboard until you answer): tap Going, Maybe or Can't go, and use + / − to say how many guests you're bringing. Parents answer for their kids. Tap your answer again to take it back, or tap another one if plans change. For these events your RSVP takes the place of "Got it." Only the managers see the headcount and who said what — your castmates don't. Managers: RSVP turns on by itself for party and potluck events; tick 🎉 Ask for an RSVP when adding or editing any other event if you want a headcount (or untick it to turn it off). Rehearsals and performances don't ask, on purpose — those are calls, not invitations.

Running late or sick on rehearsal day?

Open the event on your Schedule tab (available from 24 hours before) and tap 🕒 Running late or 😷 Can't make it, add a note if you like, and your managers are alerted immediately by email (and text, where enabled). No hunting for the stage manager's number. Parents see the same buttons for each child they manage — the alert tells the managers it's about your child, reported by you.

Why do names in chat look like "Anna K. (Golde)"?

Inside a show, everyone's messages are signed with their first name, last initial, and their role or character — so you always know whether that's Anna from the ensemble or Anna the stage manager. On the community board it's just the name.

How do I reply, edit, delete, or send a GIF?

Tap the + under any message for reactions plus ↩ Reply (your message shows a quote of the one you're answering — tap the quote to jump to it). On your own messages you'll also see ✎ Edit and 🗑 Delete; edited messages say "edited" so nobody is misled about what was said. Managers can delete any message in their show. To send a GIF, tap GIF next to the 📷, search, and tap one — you can add words before you send. GIFs come from GIPHY and only G-rated ones are allowed, because our shows have kids in them. If your phone is set to reduce motion, GIFs stay still until you tap ▶ Play. Messages are grouped by day with the time beside each name, and Show earlier messages at the top loads older conversation.

How do polls work?

Anyone can start a poll right in a show channel: tap 📊 next to the GIF button. There are two kinds. A question with answers ("Cast party — pizza or tacos?") lets people pick one answer, or several if the manager allows it. A When works? poll lists dates and times you're considering — like a Doodle poll — and everyone marks each one Works or Maybe; the time most people can make is marked ★ Best so far. Everyone who can read that channel can vote and see the results, including who picked what, so a poll in a 🔒 channel stays inside it. Tap your choice again to take it back. Kids under 13 don't have their own accounts, so a parent answers for themselves. Whoever started the poll (or a manager) can close it when the decision's made, and managers can 📌 Pin to the Dashboard so an open poll shows on everyone's landing page — closing it takes it off.

How is the Messages page laid out?

Like the messaging app on your phone. Conversations lists the show's channels, each with its latest message and time; a dot and bold text mean something new arrived since you last looked (remembered per device). Under Private are your 🔒 channel with the managers and, for adults, 💬 direct messages. Tap a conversation to open it full width; ← All conversations (or your phone's back button) takes you back to the list.

How do I mute a chatty channel?

Open the conversation and tap 🔔 Mute under its name. You can still read it any time — muting only affects notifications.

Who can read which channel?

Channels with a 🔒 are locked, and the lock is enforced by the server — not just hidden from view. 🔒 Cast is performers (and parents of young performers) plus the managers; 🔒 Crew is the same for crew; 🔒 Production team is for whoever is in the show's Production team group (music director, choreographer and the like — managers add them under Manage → Groups) plus the managers; and 🔒 Managers is only for the people with Manage access. Channels without a lock — like All Call and Off Topic — are for everyone in the show. Notifications follow the same rules: a locked channel's messages only ever ping the people who can read it. If you don't see a channel someone mentions, that's the lock doing its job.

For directors & stage managers

What does it cost?

For Grays Harbor productions: nothing, ever. For cast, crew, families, and everyone who just participates: nothing, ever, anywhere. Theater companies outside Grays Harbor chip in a small amount that covers what the service costs to run — cost recovery, not profit. Curious what Glow Tape could do for your company? See the director's overview.

Does Glow Tape use AI? (Or: "I don't like AI.")

Fair question, and the answer is no: Glow Tape doesn't use AI as part of what it does. Nothing in the app is generated, recommended, or moderated by AI, and none of your information — messages, photos, schedules, anything — is collected for AI, sold, or used to train AI. Your production's business stays your production's business. In the spirit of being straight with you: the app itself was built with a combination of human and AI resources, with a person deciding what it does and checking how it works. Once it's running, it's just software — there's no AI inside.

One honest caveat: your show's production team may use AI tools of their own, outside Glow Tape — to draft the rehearsal calendar or the show breakdown, say — and that can include show information they already see as the team. That's their choice, under their AI tool's own policies. If you'd like to know how your show does it, just ask your director.

Getting your show onto Glow Tape

On your home screen, tap Request a production and answer a few questions — theater, title, timeline, whether kids are involved. The organizer reviews it (usually quickly) and you'll get an email with your join code and the Manage tab ready to go.

Setting up a new show, step by step

Your To-Do tab opens with a setup checklist (managers only) that walks you from approval to opening auditions to the full rehearsal schedule — and checks itself off as you go. Prefer paper? Print the one-page Director's Setup Guide for the production binder.

Where's my join code?

Manage tab → Invite people. Share the big dashed code at the read-through, print the read-through handout and write it in, or tap 📱 Show QR code and let people scan it off your phone (or a printout) — scanning lands them on signup with the code already filled in. Auditions have their own QR under Manage → Auditions.

Can I invite someone with a link?

Yes — in Manage, tap 📤 Share invite link (or 📤 next to any role) and text or email it. Opening the link fills the code in automatically: new folks sign up and land straight in the show; signed-in folks just join.

Which events show up on the community calendar?

Anything with an Auditions or Performance event type is listed publicly — title, time, and place only, never who's called or your notes. The event form shows a 🌍 note whenever you're creating one, so it's never a surprise.

Can people buy tickets from the community calendar?

Yes. In Manage → Tickets, paste a link to buy tickets for your show, and every Performance gets a 🎟 Buy tickets button on the community calendar. If your company has a standing box-office link (set once by the operator), it's used as a fallback whenever a show doesn't set its own. No link either way, no button — nothing you have to fill in.

A chat just for one group

Manage → Channels: when adding a channel, pick 🔒 Group A + managers only (any of your groups). Only that group's members — parents of any children in it included — and the managers can see the channel or its messages, enforced on the server. Handy for "Group A blocking notes" without the whole cast scrolling past it.

Can an AI help me build the breakdown and the sheets?

Yes — outside Glow Tape, on your own terms. This page has a ready-made prompt: attach your script to an AI chat (like Claude), paste the prompt, and you get back six CSVs that the Sheets tab imports directly — the show breakdown (every song or scene, act, pages, who's on stage) plus starter Props, Costumes, Set, and Light/Sound cue sheets. They're drafts to edit, not gospel. Glow Tape itself still contains no AI and never sees your script; you're just outsourcing the highlighter work, then checking it.

I already have a planning spreadsheet full of to-dos

Bring it along. Export it as a CSV (Google Sheets: File → Download → CSV) and use ⬆ Import tasks from CSV at the bottom of the To-Do tab. Columns are matched by name — What (or Task), Type, Who, Due, Done — so the classic planning-workbook layout imports as-is: the Type column becomes the section headings, dates come through, and checked-off rows arrive already done. Who is matched against roles and names; initials or people not in the show just import unassigned (tasks never need an assignee — assign them whenever). There's an Export CSV button too, so the board can leave with you.

The Cast and Crew channels are locked group channels

Every production comes with Cast and Crew groups that fill themselves in automatically — cast someone as a performer and they're in the Cast group (and the 🔒 Cast channel) the moment you save; move them to crew and they move too. You can't delete these two groups, but you can rename them, put them on breakdown units, or call them when scheduling like any other group.

Role groups (Group A, Leads, Dancers…)

Manage → Groups: name your groups, then in People & roles tap the group chips under each role — a role can be in several groups. From then on, picking a group on a breakdown unit or when adding a rehearsal calls everyone in it. Change a group later and the breakdown's ↻ Re-sync button updates upcoming events.

A rehearsal that's two things at once

When adding an event, the type is now pick-all-that-apply — a night can be Dance + Vocals or Blocking + Dialogue. On the Schedule tab, the type filter shows just one kind of rehearsal (combined nights count), and the print button then gives you a paper schedule for exactly that — a vocals schedule, a dance schedule.

Scheduling a whole rehearsal run at once

In Manage → Add to the schedule, check Repeats weekly, tap the weekdays, set the end date. Up to 60 events are created and the cast gets one summary email.

Fixing up several events at once

Manage → Fix up the schedule is a table of upcoming events — change types, titles, days, times, places in bulk, then Save all. Everyone called gets one email covering everything that actually moved; fixing typos bothers nobody.

Event types and preset places

Manage → Event types & places. Customize the event-type list (Table Read, Blocking, Dance…) and save your usual rehearsal places with street addresses — locations then autofill when scheduling, show up as tappable map links, and ride along into people's calendars. Places saved on your theater's organization are shared across its productions.

Meeting on Zoom (or Meet, Teams…)

When you add or edit an event, paste the meeting link into the Zoom or video link box. Leave Location empty for an online-only meeting, or fill in both when some people are in the room and others join from home. (Paste the link into Location by mistake and Glow Tape moves it to the video box for you.) Everyone called gets a Join button in the app, the link in the schedule email and their reminders, and changing the link counts as a place change — "Got it"s reset and people are told. The community calendar only ever says Online; the link itself stays inside your show.

Archiving a show when the run is over

Manage → Archive this show. Archiving tucks the production into a Past shows list on everyone's home screen and locks it as a read-only record — no new events, messages, or edits, and the Casting tab is put away. Nothing is deleted: the schedule, messages, notes, and people all stay exactly as they were to look back on. Tap Unarchive this show anytime to reopen it. Only managers can archive or unarchive.

Announcements

Manage → Post an announcement for the things everyone must see — it's pinned atop the Messages tab and emailed to the whole production, with a "Got it" so you can see who's read it.

Texting the whole cast

Manage → Text the cast for urgent logistics (a room change, a cancellation). Pick The cast or Everyone in the show, write up to 300 characters, and send. Anyone who said yes to text reminders gets a text; everyone else gets it as a private Glow Tape message in their 🔒 channel with the managers, plus an email. People who haven't joined Glow Tape yet get the email if you've saved one for them in People & roles. Afterwards you'll see who was texted, who got it in the app, who was emailed, and who couldn't be reached. To keep people trusting these, a show can send at most 3 a day and none between 9pm and 7am — use an announcement for everything else.

Seeing the schedule through one person's eyes

On the Schedule tab, pick a name under View as… to see exactly what that cast member sees — handy for "wait, why does Emma think she's off Thursday?" — and print their personal schedule (list + monthly calendars) from the same spot.

When can Group A rehearse? (finding a time)

At the bottom of the Schedule tab (managers only), 🗓 When can we rehearse? answers the eternal question. Pick who — everyone, one of your role groups, or hand-picked people — a time slot (say 6:00–9:00pm), and a date window. You get a grid like a Doodle poll: one row per person, one column per day, ✓ where they're free and ✕ where something they entered gets in the way (tap-and-hold or hover a ✕ to see why — "work Mon–Fri", "vacation"). The bottom row counts who's free, and ★ marks the days the most people can make; 🎭 means something's already scheduled that day. The grid scrolls sideways if the window is long.

Conflict lists go stale, so the last step is asking. Tap days in the grid (or ★ Pick the best days), choose a channel — it starts on the group's own 🔒 channel if you picked a group — and Post the poll. It lands as a 📅 When works? poll where everyone marks Works or Maybe, and ▦ See everyone's answers as a grid on the poll shows who said what at a glance. If you hand-picked people, only they (and their parents) get a notification — everyone else in the channel can still see the poll — and the poll lists who it's still waiting on. The better the cast is about entering conflicts, the better the starting grid gets — nudge them early.

The Casting tab

Your private casting worksheet — invisible to everyone but the managers. Draft the cast with dropdowns fed by audition signups, people already in the show (🎭, at the bottom of each list), or ✍ someone from a paper form — type their name and it's recorded on the role as a not-on-Glow-Tape member. Double-cast picks get a ⚠ flag and each pick's conflicts show inline, so you can see problems while you think (nothing has to be resolved to keep drafting). When you're sure, Finalize the cast — after a you-are-sure warning — and everyone drafted is attached to their role and sees the show. Casting a lead or two before the rest (Jesus and Judas first)? ✓ Cast now next to any pick locks in just that role while the rest of your draft stays private. Child roles are still claimed by parents with their role codes. Bonus: the conflict dates people entered on their audition form are copied into the Schedule tab's conflicts the moment they're cast — your stage manager starts with everyone's unavailable dates already in hand.

Cast members who won't use the app

In Manage → People & roles, tap 📇 Not on Glow Tape? on their role and record their name, email, and phone. They'll get the production's emails, appear on your contact sheet, and never get texts (they didn't opt in).

Roll call (taking attendance)

On any event, tap Roll call and tap names to cycle present → late → missing. People who told you ahead (“running late” or “can't make it”) sit in their own 📞 Called in group at the top (their names show even while it's folded; tap it to see each one in purple with their note), so they never get mixed up with someone who's just missing (red, dashed). If a called-in person shows up anyway, tap their name to mark them here. If someone texted or called you instead of using the app, mark them late or missing, then tap 📞 Called in next to their name to move them into that group. The event card shows running totals. Better yet, let the cast take their own roll: inside roll call, tap 📲 Turn on door check-in to get a QR code — show it on your phone or tap 🖨 Door poster for a printable page to tape by the door. Cast scan it, tap one big ✋ I'm here button (parents check in their kids too), and they show up in your roll call as present with their arrival time. Tap ↻ Refresh roll to pick up the latest scans, and turn check-in off afterwards if you like — old posters simply stop working. Got a spare old phone? Prop it by the door open to the poster page and tap 📺 Kiosk mode: the screen stays awake and everything but the QR hides. Pin the screen so taps can't wander (Android: Settings → Security → App pinning; iPhone: Guided Access) and keep it plugged in. Manage → Attendance report (managers only) lists every rehearsal's roll call, newest first: tap one to see who was late, missing, or called in, with their notes. Below that is the season-long tally per person. Attendance is never emailed. On days with something scheduled, the managers still get a short nightly rehearsal report email at 10pm with overdue to-dos and the notes written that day (no email if there's nothing to say).

Planning a rehearsal minute by minute (the timeline)

Any event can carry a run of show: tap ⏱ Plan the timeline (managers only) and list what happens with how long it takes — cast meeting 5 min, warmups 10, work a scene 30, run it 20, notes 15. You never type clock times: they're worked out from the event's start, so stretching one part shifts everything after it, and the editor warns if the plan runs past the event's end. Start from a built-in template — Rehearsal, Performance night (call, warmups, sound check, places, curtain), or Tech — or save your own arrangement as a named template and reuse it all season. Everyone sees the finished timeline on the event (⏱ Timeline), and it's on the printed schedule too, so "when do I actually need to be warmed up" stops being a question.

Show reports (performance nights)

Every performance-type event has a 📋 Show report button (managers only) — the stage manager's classic performance report as a two-minute phone form after curtain call: audience count, house-open and curtain times, anything technical that went sideways, injuries or incidents, and how it went. Saving it emails all the managers that night, the way the paperwork has always traveled. Come back to the same button to edit; past performances keep theirs under Show past events. Cast never see show reports.

What happens when I edit or cancel an event?

Changing the date, time, or place automatically emails everyone called and resets their "Got it" so you can see who's seen the change. Cancelling emails everyone and crosses it out (it can be restored). Fixing a typo in the title or notes doesn't bother anyone.

Can I build the schedule before auditions?

Yes. In Manage → People & roles, add the roles themselves (Ophelia, Ensemble, Light Board Op) before anyone is cast, and call those roles on events as you build the schedule. Each role gets a claim code (the join code plus two letters, like TR7PDQ-XY). On cast night, hand each person their role's code — when they join with it, their entire schedule is already waiting for them. If you saved their email on the role, the plain show code works too: it finds the role by that email.

The To-Do tab

Simple task lists by department — set build, props, costumes, publicity. Managers add tasks with an optional assignee and due date (assignees get one email); whoever it's assigned to checks it off. Overdue items turn red. That's the whole system, on purpose. (When bios are requested, each person's bio box appears at the top of their To-Do tab.)

Holding auditions

Manage → Auditions: add the playwright/composer credit, describe the show (both land at the top of the signup form), write the details (dates, place, what to prepare) and any questions of your own, one per line, and preview the signup form — all before anyone else can see it. What each role calls for — gender, age range, character — is written on the role itself in People & roles, and appears under that role's checkbox on the form (and the paper form). When it looks right, check the box to open signups: every Glow Tape user sees the show on their home screen and can sign up; signups collect right there in Manage — each one shows their answers, conflicts, and community profile (headshot, experience, skills). No emails fly around; check the list whenever you like. Uncheck the box to close auditions. Name your performer roles in People & roles first and they appear on the signup form as checkboxes (and on the paper form as check-off boxes), so you learn who wants what. Audition-type events from your schedule show on the form automatically — and so do your Performance and Strike events, as the commitment: signups must check a box confirming they're available for all of them (the answer shows on each signup you review). For the paper crowd, Print blank paper forms gives you a hand-fillable version with the same questions, and sides or packets can be attached under Documents & links (area: Auditions) so every auditioner can open them.

Cutting audition sides from the script

Sides — the short excerpts people read at auditions — can be cut straight from any PDF in the script room (managers only). Scroll to the bottom, tap ✂️ Cut audition sides from this script, name the side ("Side 1 — Bankers scene"), and list the pages ("34-36", or "12, 34-36" for a jump). A new little PDF appears with the audition materials automatically, so everyone who opens the signup form can read it — no full script handed out, no photocopier. Mind that the page numbers are the viewer's own count, which can differ from the numbers printed on the page.

Sharing documents and links

Manage → Documents & links. Give it a title, then paste a link or attach a file (PDFs, tracks — up to 50 MB), and choose whether it's for the show (cast and crew see it on the Docs tab) or for auditions (it appears on the signup form). Some documents aren't for the whole cast — pick 🔒 Managers only and only managers can see or open it (enforced on the server, not just hidden). The 🔒/🌐 button next to each document switches it later.

The Notes tab (rehearsal notes)

After a run-through, tap + New note — the title is pre-filled with the date — and type your notes. Then pick a channel and tap 💬 Post to chat: the note appears in the channel as a tappable link showing its title. (🔗 Copy link is there too if you'd rather paste it yourself.) Anyone in the show can write notes; you (and the managers) can edit yours later, and everyone can read them all on the Notes tab. The search box above the list looks inside every note — type "fainting couch" and see exactly which nights it came up.

The show breakdown (Sheets → Breakdown)

Break the show into units and scheduling gets smart. If you change who's in a unit after events are scheduled, tap ↻ Re-sync the schedule on the Breakdown to update the call lists on upcoming events (quietly — nobody is emailed). Pick the shape that fits: by song for a musical (each song tracks who's on stage, who sings, who dances), by scene, or by pages/hunks for a straight play. Build it by hand — tap a unit, toggle the roles involved — or import a CSV with columns like Song, Act, Pages, On stage, Singing, Dancing, Notes; people are matched by their role or character name from People & roles, so it works before anyone is cast. Then, when adding to the schedule, pick the units you're rehearsing and everyone involved is called automatically. Cast members can tap Just mine to see exactly which songs or scenes are theirs.

The Sheets tab (props, costumes, set, cues)

This replaces the multi-tab production Google Sheet. Pick a sheet — Props, Costumes, Set, Light cues, Sound cues — and managers edit right in the table: tap a cell, type, tap away, it's saved. Everyone in the show can read them; only managers can edit. Moving off an old spreadsheet? In Google Sheets do File → Download → Comma Separated Values (one tab at a time), then tap ⬆ Import CSV on the matching sheet — columns are matched by their headers. ⬇ Export CSV gets your data back out any time, and 🖨 Print makes paper copies for the booth. Column too narrow? Drag the edge of its heading wider or narrower (on a keyboard, focus the edge and use the left/right arrows). Widths are remembered on that phone or computer just for you, and ↔ Reset column widths puts them back.

The program & publicity packet

Most companies have a program/brochure person who asks the director for the same packet every show: cast and characters, production team, bios, a description, the playwright, audition and performance dates, the song list. Glow Tape already knows nearly all of it — Manage → Program bios → 📦 Program & publicity packet compiles the whole thing, and 📋 Copy it all for email puts it on your clipboard as plain text ready to paste and send. The few things only you know (play type, rating, licensing house, acknowledgments) appear as fill-in blanks so nothing gets forgotten. Ads, design, and company-side credits stay with the company — this is just the director's homework, done.

Collecting program bios

Manage → Program bios → Request bios from the cast. Everyone missing a bio gets a to-do (and an email; guardians get their child's). People write theirs right on the To-Do tab, it checks off their task automatically, and the compiled page gives you the whole program section to print or copy in one tap.

No bios this time? Some shows don't print them. Manage → Program bios → check This show has no program bios, and Glow Tape stops asking: no bio nudges on the Dashboard, no bio box on To-Do, and no bios in the program packet. Uncheck it to turn bios back on — anything already written is kept.

Who gets the Manage tab?

Anyone with the Manage box checked in Manage → People & roles. It checks itself automatically for directors, assistant directors, and stage managers (including when they claim a staff role code), and you can grant it to anyone else — producer, choreographer, music director. Managers see the Manage tab, everyone's conflicts, acknowledgment counts, and roll call.

Who else can see our production?

One person: the Glow Tape Stagehand — the app's operator (a real, named person, not a company). The Stagehand can step into any production with manager-level access to help with setup and troubleshooting, the same way a landlord holds a spare key. Two things the Stagehand still can not see: anyone's personal script-room highlights and drawings (those are private to each person, full stop), and anything that doesn't exist — Glow Tape has no analytics, no trackers, and no AI built in. If the Stagehand posts in your show's messages, the name is labeled "(Glow Tape Stagehand)" so nobody wonders who that is.

🛡️ Kids & families

How do children join a show?

They don't sign up — a parent or guardian does. The director adds the child as a role in Manage (checking "This is a child"), which creates a code. A parent claims that code (or its invite link) with their own account, and from then on the parent sees the child's full schedule, gets every reminder, taps "Got it," and reads every group message the child's cast can see. Children under 13 never have logins — that's by design, not a limitation. Read more: Youth Safety at Glow Tape.

Both parents want the schedule.

Share the same child code with both — every guardian who claims it independently gets everything. Grandparents driving to rehearsal too, if you like.

Photos of kids

Every member has a photo-consent setting (Manage → People & roles → 📷). If anyone in a production has photos turned off, the app reminds everyone before pictures get posted. When in doubt, don't post faces.

How do I report a worrying message?

Tap the + under any message, then the red ⚑ Report button. It goes straight to the Glow Tape operator — a real person, and deliberately not the production's own managers — who will look at it promptly. Use it for anything that feels off; you won't get anyone in trouble by asking a human to take a look.

Can I message someone privately?

Only between two adults. If you and another person in your show are both 18 or older, open Messages, tap 💬 Message someone privately under Private, pick their name, and tap 💬 Start. A direct message is just the two of you — managers can't read it — and it has the usual chat extras (photos, GIFs, replies, editing and deleting your own messages).

  • Never with anyone under 18. Teen accounts don't see direct messages at all, and the server refuses them even if someone tries to get around the app. Children under 13 never have accounts. Teens become eligible automatically once their account says they've turned 18.
  • Only people who share a show. A direct message lives inside that show. If either person leaves the show, nobody can send in it any more.
  • Block anyone. Open the conversation and tap 🚫 Block. Neither of you can send direct messages to the other until you unblock them, and they aren't told.
  • Report anything. The ⚑ button works in direct messages too, and the report goes to the Glow Tape operator, not the show's managers.

Anything involving a minor still happens in group channels, where no adult can privately message a child — the same open-communication standard used by youth sports and scouting nationwide.

Ideas, problems, wishes

On your home screen, find Ideas & feedback, pick a flavor (idea, something broken, question, something nice), and send it. Every submission is read by a human, and you'll see its status — received, planned, done — right under the form on later visits.
